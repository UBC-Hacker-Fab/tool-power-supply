## Decided

### Design principle: minimize the BOM
Prefer one part family or footprint that serves several branches over a different scheme per branch. Count unique extended parts, since JLC charges a fee per unique extended part. Through-hole parts that are easy to hand-solder are acceptable and can be sourced from Digikey/Mouser. SMD fuses were rejected as annoying to swap.

### Planning assumption (2026-10-11): no bench measurements before the build
The user cannot scope anything before rev 1 is built, so risks that I proposed to settle by measurement are handled by defensive design instead: populate the protection, keep fallbacks as footprints, ask vendors for numbers. Affected items: ESC back-feed (series Schottky), module inrush (4 A time-lag fuse option plus NTC footprint, ask Mean Well for I2t), SSR drive (use the MOSFET, since direct drive can't be bench-checked at 3.3 V).

### AC/DC conversion
- **Mean Well IRM-90-12, LCSC C6577385** is the on-board AC/DC module.
  - 12 V / 6.7 A / 80 W, 85-305 VAC (80-305 VAC per LCSC), ~92% efficient.
  - 87 x 52 x 29.5 mm, through-hole 4-DIP, CB/CE/cURus/EAC. EasyEDA footprint exists.
  - JLC extended part. Live stock 2,315 on 2026-10-06.
  - LCSC datasheet: https://wmsc.lcsc.com/wmsc/upload/file/pdf/v2/lcsc/2404091558_MW-MEAN-WELL-Enterprises-IRM-90-12_C6577385.pdf
  - Fallback: IRM-60-12 (C6577383, 60 W), same footprint.
- **No 24 V bus.** Nothing in the tool set needs it. 12 V comes directly from the module, so there is no 12 V buck stage.
- **Custom AC/DC is out of scope for rev 1.** If revisited, do it on a separate board. Starting points: Power Integrations AN-72 and DER-868/DER-712, TI UCC28740.

### Power budget
- **Sized for one tool at a time.** Worst case is the spin coater: ESC 12 V / 5 A (~60 W) plus ~10 W of logic and the 5 V / 3.3 V rails, ~70 W, ~5.9 A of the 6.7 A available.
- Tap-only case: 2 x 12 V taps at 1 A nominal / 2 A peak = 4 A peak (~48 W).
- Single-tool operation is **not enforced in hardware**. ESC 5 A plus both taps at peak (9 A) exceeds 6.7 A. Label the board with the 6.7 A total limit.
- **Tube furnace (decided 2026-10-10):** CMU's Tube Furnace 4 (Aphex) page says the heater should draw no more than 1000 W (https://docs.hackerfab.org/home/fab-toolkit/annealing/tube-furnace/tube-furnace-4-aphex). At 120 VAC that is ~8.3 A. Size the AC passthrough, terminal and traces for that. The page does not state supply voltage or thermocouple type.

### Tube furnace SSR: Fotek SSR-60 DA (user's part)
Specs below are from web search summaries of the Fotek SSR-60DA datasheet; the datasheet page itself returned HTTP 403, so verify against the unit. Sources disagree on the output range (the label in the user's photo says 24-380 VAC; some listings say 24-480 VAC). Fotek-branded SSRs are widely cloned, so treat the 60 A rating as unverified.
- Output 24-380 VAC (label), 60 A max with heat sink. Input 3-32 VDC, **12 mA max input current**. Off-state leakage <= 2 mA. Turn-on < 10 ms. Zero-cross type, so control it with time-proportional (burst) control, not phase-angle.
- **On-state drop <= 1.5 V.** At 8.3 A that is up to **~12.5 W** dissipated in the SSR (~9 W at a typical 1.1 V). Assuming a base temperature limit of ~80 C and 40 C ambient (my assumption, not from the datasheet), the heat sink needs to be roughly <= 3 C/W. A PCB copper pour alone will not do this.
- **Decided (2026-10-11): SSR off-board on a heat sink.** The board provides fused L / N / PE out, a 2-position SSR control terminal, and a small low-side driver (N-MOSFET plus gate resistor) so the Pico does not drive the 12 mA input directly. The SSR is wired externally in the heater's hot lead.

### Scope
- **Litho (CMU) is out of scope for rev 1** (decided 2026-10-07). Its 19 V / 4.74 A (~90 W) projector supply can't come from the 12 V module and exceeds 80 W, so it keeps its own adapters. Its 12 V / 5 A shield supply would fit the existing 12 V rail if it's ever brought in. Rev 1 targets the spin coater and tube furnace only, plus general 12 V / 5 V / 3.3 V taps for future tools.

### 5 V / 3.3 V taps: non-isolated, protected (decided 2026-10-07, protection part updated 2026-10-11)
- **Not isolated.** The IRM-90-12 already gives reinforced mains isolation and its output floats, so a DC/DC adds no safety. Ground-loop and ESC-noise concerns are handled by layout (single PE tie at the board, star ground, separate ESC and logic returns) and an LC/ferrite filter ahead of each buck, not by isolation.
- Per rail: one buck from 12 V (AP63205 / AP63203), then **one TPS2553 per tap (4 total)**. A TVS sits on the 12 V rail for ESC back-feed.
- **TPS2561 (dual) was rejected**: its datasheet only lists a reverse leakage spec and has no reverse-voltage protection (user confirmed, and my grep agrees). TPS2553 has an internal reverse-voltage comparator that disables the switch when OUT exceeds IN by ~135 mV (95-190 mV), ~5 ms response, and asserts FAULT. It is still one unique part.
- **Fixed vs adjustable buck (checked 2026-10-13):** kept the fixed AP63205 / AP63203 over one adjustable part for both rails. The adjustable AP63200WU-7 (LCSC C2071868, extended, 2,923 in stock, listed ~$2.01 vs ~$0.42 for the AP63205 and ~$1.18 for the AP63203) switches at 500 kHz instead of 1.1 MHz (so likely a larger inductor; AP63200 datasheet table not read), and needs a feedback divider per rail (0.8 V reference): 5 V is 10 k + ~52.5 k, 3.3 V is 10 k + ~31.3 k, which are not basic-library values; with basic E24 values (51 k / 30 k) the outputs land about 2-3% low. Saves one unique extended part fee but adds resistors and loses accuracy. Reconsider if rail voltages need to change in a later rev. AP63201 (adjustable) has almost no stock.
- Rev 2 idea: a footprint option for one isolated tap if a sensitive measurement (tube furnace thermocouple front end) needs it.

### Fuses and protection (decided 2026-10-11)
- **12 V DC branches (ESC and 2 taps): blade fuses in PCB-mount holders**, through-hole and easy to swap, sourced from Digikey/Mouser. One holder part, two fuse values: **3 A** (2 x 12 V taps) and **7.5 A** (ESC; ESC runs 5 A continuous, and 7.5 A sits just above the module's 7.37 A / 10 s peak). Automotive blade fuses are rated 32 V, so they are **only for the 12 V DC branches, never for the 120 VAC branches**.
- **120 VAC branches (module input and furnace output): 6.3 x 32 mm (3AG) cartridge fuses in through-hole clips**, one clip part for both: **3 A time-lag** (module branch, for the 30 A cold-start inrush) and **10 A ceramic fast-blow, 250 VAC** (furnace branch). **The Slo-Blo 313 series is only 32 V AC at 10 A and 12 A (checked on Digikey), so it can't be used on 120 VAC at the furnace current.** A 10 A fuse on an ~8.3 A heater is 83% loading, tight but acceptable for a resistive load with little inrush.
- **3AG fuse footprint (decided 2026-10-14): one symbol and one footprint for the whole class of 6.3 x 32 mm cartridge fuses that fit the Littelfuse 102/122 clips.** The HF library has `HF_Passives:Fuse_3AG_6.3x32mm` with footprint `HF_Footprints:Fuse_3AG_6.3x32mm_Clips_Littelfuse_102_122_P34.21mm`: four pads, pads 1+1 are the two pins of one clip and 2+2 the other, 7.62 mm between a clip's pins and 34.21 mm outer pin to outer pin (datasheet dimension A = 1.347 in for a 1-1/4 in fuse), 1.98 mm holes, fuse body drawn on the fab layer, two 01020074Z 3D models. Dimensions are the Littelfuse 102/122 datasheet's and match KiCad's stock `Fuse:Fuseholder_Clip-6.3x32mm_Littelfuse_102_122_Inline_P34.21x7.62mm_D1.98mm_Horizontal` pad layout. **Any 3AG fuse in the schematic (312, 313, 314, 326, 505, 506, 508 series) uses this symbol, so F1 and F2 are real board parts and the separate clip components (old XF1 to XF4) are gone.** The clips are still bought separately (2 per fuse, 01020074Z), which is recorded in each fuse's `Clip MPN` field and in the BOM. The fuse is a consumable and is not soldered. Not covered: 5 x 20 mm fuses and the blade fuses (those have their own holders).
- **Blade fuses use the same one-part approach (decided 2026-10-15).** F4, F5 (3 A) and F6 (7.5 A) are `HF_Passives:Fuse_Blade_Keystone_3557` with the Keystone 3557-2 / 3557-20 holder as the footprint (`HF_Footprints:Fuse_Blade_Keystone_3557-20_P13.47mm`, pads 1+1 and 2+2). The separate holder symbols XH1 to XH3 are gone. Any future ATO/ATC blade fuse in that holder uses this symbol.
- **Value field convention (decided 2026-10-15).** Passives show only the standard unit value (`33k`, `100nF`, `4.7uH`, `5m` for a 5 mOhm shunt, `3A`, `500mA`); everything else is a hidden field (`Tolerance`, `Voltage`, `Dielectric`, `Type`, `Function`, `MPN`, `LCSC`, ...). ICs, transistors, diodes and LEDs use the MPN as the value (`AO4407C`, `INA226AIDGSR`, `SC1631` for the Pico 2). Connectors, jumpers and mounting holes keep descriptive values. The MOV shows its MPN (`10D241K`). Passives use the small KiCad symbols (`R_Small_US`, `C_Small`, `C_Polarized_Small`, `L_Small`, `Polyfuse_Small`; thermistor and varistor have no small version).
- **Board size and EMI/EMC approach (2026-10-15).** The tube furnace mechanical design gave a conservative 120 x 80 mm limit; the BOM does not fit (see `pcb-placement.md`), so the user chose **90 x 160 mm** (portrait). EMI/EMC: no board-level mains filter and no Y cap, because the IRM-90-12 is Class II with built-in EMI filtering and is listed (by distributors; Mean Well datasheet not read) as meeting EN 55032 Class B; the optional X2 cap stays off. The furnace SSR is zero-crossing and switches slowly. Noise control is layout: the two 1.1 MHz bucks (the AP63xxx family uses frequency spread spectrum per the Diodes product page) get tight input-cap and switch-node loops and a solid ground return, and the ESC (kHz PWM, amps of ripple) gets C3 close to the XT60 and a loop kept away from the shunts and the Pico. A ferrite clamp on the ESC and motor leads is off-board. TVS or clamps only on the cable-facing signal ports that need them (thermistor terminal, USB). SJ3 (PE to GND tie) is the common-mode return choice; try it open and closed when measuring. Not designed for certification.
- **Hand edits in KiCad (noticed 2026-10-15).** The schematic on disk now differs from `tools/schematic/design.py`: the ESC connector J5 had become a DB128V screw terminal by mistake and was changed back to the XT60PW-F on 2026-10-15; parts were re-annotated. Re-annotated again on 2026-10-15 (the user ran the annotator after the schematic cleanup). Current designators: F1 furnace 10 A and F2 module 3 A (3AG); F3 fan PTC; F4, F5 12 V tap blade fuses and F6 ESC blade fuse; J1 AC in, J2 AC out, J3 fan header, J4, J5 12 V taps, J6 XT60 (ESC), J7, J8 3.3 V taps, J9, J10 5 V taps, J11, J12 Pico-side terminals; Q1 to Q3 AO4407C (taps A, B, ESC) and Q4 to Q6 their 2N7002 drivers, Q7 SSR drive; U3 12 V INA226 (0x40), U4 5 V buck, U5 3.3 V buck, U6 5 V INA226 (0x41), U7 3.3 V INA226 (0x42), U8, U9 5 V TPS2553 and U10, U11 3.3 V TPS2553; R22 and R23 the 33 mOhm shunts; C3 the 100 uF bulk cap; SJ1 to SJ3 chassis/PE jumpers and SJ4, SJ5 the SSR jumpers. Older notes in this file may use the previous numbering; the live source is the schematic and `tools/power_tree/power_model.json`, and an MOV curve image was added.
- **Mains LED dropper check (2026-10-15).** 4 x 33k = 132k across 120 VAC rms: 0.91 mA rms, 0.11 W total, 27 mW per resistor (27% of 0.1 W), 42 V peak per resistor (Uni-Royal 0603 working voltage about 75 V, UNVERIFIED). LED half-wave peak 1.3 mA, average 0.4 mA (0.8 mW); the 1N4148W carries the other half. Fine; it is 0.11 W of constant standby loss.
- **ACS712 FILTER pin (2026-10-15).** 100 nF with the typical internal RF of 1.7 kOhm gives 1/(2 pi x 1.7k x 100n) = 0.94 kHz. The datasheet's own rise-time table for 100 nF (291 us, f = 0.35 / tr) implies about 1.2 kHz, so the real -3 dB point is roughly 0.9 to 1.2 kHz (RF(INT) has no tolerance in the datasheet). The earlier '940 Hz' was only the first number.
- **Inductor and shunt annotations (2026-10-15).** SLO0630H4R7MTT: Isat 9.0 A (20% drop), Irms 5.5 A, DCR 38 mOhm max (Sunltech datasheet). AP63203/AP63205 switch at 1.1 MHz typ with a 3.1 A max high-side limit, so ripple is about 0.56 A (5 V) and 0.46 A (3.3 V) pk-pk at 2 A and the inductor has about 2.9x margin. Shunt drops are in `power-tree.md` (section 'Shunt voltage drop') and written next to R5, R20, R30 on the schematic; the worst case is 66 mV on the 33 mOhm shunts at a 2 A buck limit, against the INA226 range of 81.92 mV.
- **12 V eFuse (TPS259570) dropped.** Per `datasheets/tps2595.pdf`: latch-off, no output overvoltage clamp, active-high enable, 2.7-18 V operating (20 V abs max). Each branch would also need an ILIM resistor and a dVdT cap, and its 4 A limit is below the ESC's 5 A. Telemetry comes from INA226 monitors.
- **SMD fuses (Littelfuse 0154 family) were dropped** because they are annoying to swap. The 125 V rating and 50 A breaking capacity were also a concern on mains; the 3AG parts above are 250 V.
- **ESC connector:** the board provides the **female** XT60 (XT60PW-F), so the ESC lead is male. **Pending (2026-10-21 design review): swap to a vertical female XT60** so nothing hangs off the board edge; see 'Control link, I/O expander, connector and mounting changes'.

### SSR drive, shunts, fuse holder (decided 2026-10-12)
- **SSR drive: 2N7002 low-side MOSFET populated by default, 5 V drive (revised by the user, 2026-10-14; was direct drive with the MOSFET not connected).** Two 3-pad solder jumpers (no BOM): SJ4 selects SSR+ = 5 V (default) or GPIO; SJ5 selects SSR- = the MOSFET drain (default) or GND. The GPIO drives the 2N7002 gate through the 1 k resistor, with the 4.7 k pulldown (C23162) so the SSR is off while the Pico boots. The SSR input runs from the 5 V rail at up to 12 mA, so the Fotek's 3 V minimum has full margin. Direct 3.3 V drive stays available by moving both jumpers, in which case set the GPIO drive strength to 12 mA and check that the real SSR turns on solidly at 3.3 V (the Fotek's minimum is 3 V, the margin is small, and clones vary).
- **INA226 shunts, sized from `datasheets/ina226.pdf`:** shunt input range +/-81.92 mV (full scale), 2.5 uV/LSB, shunt offset +/-2.5 uV typ and +/-10 uV max, gain error 0.02% typ / 0.1% max, bus up to 36 V. Calibration: CAL = 0.00512 / (Current_LSB x R_shunt), Current_LSB >= max current / 2^15. The 1% shunt tolerance dominates the error, so calibrate at bring-up.
  - **12 V rail: 5 mOhm** (TA-I RLP25FEGMR005, C393074, already in the BOM): full scale 16.4 A, 37 mV and 0.27 W at the module's 7.37 A peak, 0.5 mA/bit, offset limited to +/-2 mA. Use Current_LSB = 1 mA/bit, **CAL = 1024**. Sits upstream of the series Schottky.
  - **5 V and 3.3 V rails: 33 mOhm** (TA-I RLS12FTCR033, C912764, 1206, 1 W, 3,265 in stock, extended): full scale 2.48 A against a max rail load of about 1.9 A (two taps at up to ~0.86 A each plus the Pico, from the TPS2553 limit), about 63 mV and 0.12 W at 1.9 A, 75 uA/bit, offset limited to +/-0.3 mA. Use Current_LSB = 100 uA/bit, **CAL = 1552**. Take the buck feedback from the buck-output side of the shunt so the drop appears at the taps, not at the sensing node. This adds one unique extended part but gives 6x finer resolution than reusing 5 mOhm on these rails.
- **Thermocouple interface is not a power-board feature** (decided 2026-10-12); out of scope for this board.
- **Blade fuse holder: Keystone 3557-2** (replaces the 3558). Digikey 36-3557-2-ND: single-circuit PCB-mount block for ATC/ATO/ATT blade fuses, 30 A / 500 V, vertical, PC pin, $1.31, 208,986 in stock; Mouser 534-3557-2, $1.17, 103,971 in stock. Footprint: 4 through-hole pads (2.4 mm pad, 1.6 mm drill) at +/-6.735 mm x +/-1.7 mm; each fuse terminal uses two pads, so tie pads together per the footprint: on the 3557-20 footprint added to the HF library (CSE) pads 1+2 are one terminal and 3+4 the other; on the 3557-2 footprint it is 1+3 and 2+4. 3 needed. **CSE download: part 3557-2, CSE part ID 237823** (KiCad symbol and footprint, model quality 3). The 3557-20 (ID 362661, quality 4, Mouser 534-3557-20) has identical pad geometry if you prefer the higher-quality model. The 3558 has no CSE model.

### Grounding: solder-jumper star point (decided 2026-10-12)
PE, the DC ground and any other reference potentials meet at a single star point through solder jumpers, so each tie can be bridged or left open as needed. Default is open; bridge at bring-up (bridging PE to DC ground is the recommended final configuration for a metal enclosure so a module isolation fault trips the breaker, but it stays the user's call). Jumper candidates: PE to the star, module DC GND (12 V return) to the star, ESC return to the star, logic/analog ground (Pico, INA226, ACS712 low-voltage side) to the star, ~~M2 mounting-hole chassis to the star~~ (dropped 2026-10-21: the M3 mounting holes are floating, so SJ1's chassis tie has nothing to join and should go when the holes are rewired). Keep the jumper list minimal and label them in silkscreen; the exact net list is settled in layout. PE through the board from AC-in to AC-out is not optional and is not jumpered.

### Clearance and standards approach
- **IEC 62368-1 (UL 62368-1)** with IEC 60664-1 tables governs the mains side (creepage and clearance). **IPC-2221** governs everything below mains. **IPC-2152** for trace width on the ~10 A paths.
- Inputs: 120 VAC is ~170 V peak, OVC II, PD2, FR4 material group IIIa/IIIb (check the laminate CTI). L-N and L-PE are basic insulation; mains to anything user-accessible is reinforced.
- **Creepage/clearance numbers from IEC 60664-1:2007 (`design-guides/1923421193536807.pdf`, 2026-10-16), proposed, awaiting the user's confirmation.** 120 VAC nominal rationalizes to the 125 V row (Table F.3a); single-phase 120 V is 1.5 kV impulse at OVC II, 2.5 kV at OVC III (Table F.1); altitude <= 2000 m; PD2 (inside an enclosure); FR-4 group IIIa.
  - Clearance (Table F.2, case A, PD2): basic 0.5 mm (1.5 kV); reinforced is one impulse step up (2.5 kV) = 1.5 mm (5.1.6). OVC III reinforced would be 3.0 mm.
  - Creepage (Table F.4, 125 V, PD2): basic 0.25 mm on printed wiring material (so the clearance governs), 1.50 mm on other group III surfaces; reinforced is 2x basic (5.2.4) = 0.5 mm on the board surface (clearance governs, 1.5 mm) and 3.0 mm on other surfaces (connector bodies, under components). PD3 would be 4.8 mm. Creepage can never be less than the clearance (5.2.2.6).
  - **Proposed layout rule: 3.2 mm mains-to-anything-user-reachable** (reinforced, the 160 V row, which covers 132 V high line) for both creepage and clearance, and **1.6 mm L-to-N** (basic, working voltage). The earlier **6.4 mm figure in these docs had no source and is withdrawn.**
  - Caveats: IEC 60664-1 is the basic publication; the product standard (62368-1) has its own tables, not in the repo. The laminate's actual CTI (needs >= 175 V) is unchecked. The ACS712 package gives 4 mm creepage but is basic isolation only, per its datasheet.
- Add routed slots (>= 1 mm wide) only where 3.2 mm can't be met on the surface.
  - **Implemented in KiCad 2026-10-16** (awaiting the user's confirmation of the numbers): net classes `Mains` (L, N and the LED-dropper nodes) and `PE` in the project, and `tool-power-supply-v1.kicad_dru` with rules for mains to anything else 3.2 mm, L to N and mains to PE 1.6 mm, and an exemption inside the ACS712 (the package gives its own 4 mm creepage; pad-to-pad in air across it is 3.0 mm). The LED dropper's intermediate nodes (30 V steps) stay at the default clearance. Current placement has zero violations of these rules (DRC: only the INA226 pad gaps).
- KiCad: dedicated mains net class with the larger clearance, plus Edge.Cuts slots. Check JLC's minimum routed-slot width.

## Open questions

Ship with the 3 A Slo-Blo (0313003.HXP); keep spares, with the 4 A Slo-Blo (0313004.HXP, F2546-ND) as the swap-in if it trips. No NTC, no link footprint, no active inrush limiting. ### Powering the control board (Pico 2 datasheet section 5.5, Figures 8 and 9)
- **Yes, a Pico 2 can take external power while USB is connected.** The datasheet's recommended method is a second Schottky from the external 5 V into VSYS: the USB VBUS reaches VSYS through the on-board D1, the higher of the two wins, and the diodes stop either source back-powering the other. VSYS accepts 1.8 to 5.5 V, so the roughly 4.7 V after the diode drop is fine. Do not short VBUS to VSYS when two sources exist. The one exception is USB host mode, which needs 5 V on VBUS (the control board is a USB device, so it does not apply).
- This board already powers its own Pico this way (SS14 from the 5 V rail). Figure 9 shows a P-FET version without the diode drop (for example DMG2305UX).
- **Future note (user, 2026-10-21): design the control board to fall back to USB power if the power board fails.** The diode OR-ing above already gives the supply side of that automatically. Still to design: how the control board detects the loss (CAN heartbeat timeout; VSYS on ADC3; the Pico's VBUS-present GPIO), what it reports to the lab computer, and whether to use the Figure 9 P-FET to avoid the diode drop. ## ESC facts (`datasheets/EMAX_BLHELI_20.pdf`)
- **The PDF is the "EMAX NANO series" manual (NANO 6A/12A/20A, BLHeli), not the Simon-series 12A you are using. It is likely similar, but confirm the Simon manual.**

## Cooling (discussed 2026-10-14)
- **Heat budget (estimates):** SSR up to ~12.5 W (off-board, on its own heat sink); IRM-90-12 about 6-7 W (80 W out at ~92% efficiency, convection-rated); everything else under ~1 W each (12 V shunt ~0.27 W, 33 mOhm shunts ~0.12 W, bucks ~0.2-0.5 W, TPS2553 ~0.06 W each, ACS712 ~0.1 W, fuses a few tenths of a watt). Board total about 8-9 W.
- **Options:** (1) passive: spread heat sources, module with free convection (vents low and high), keep the electrolytic bulk cap and the Pico away from the module, copper pours and thermal vias under the shunts and bucks; (2) SSR heat sink bolted to chassis or an aluminum plate, with thermal pad; (3) forced air: a 12 V fan on a tap or a dedicated fan header with a low-side MOSFET and tach, driven from the two NTCs (module and SSR heat sink); (4) software thermal limits from the NTCs: fan on, limit SSR duty, shut down. **Decided 2026-10-14: add forced-air cooling driven by the two NTCs (options 3 and 4).** Implementation: a **4-pin PC-style fan header** (Molex 47053-1000, Mouser 538-47053-1000, $0.56, 177,093 in stock, hand-soldered): pin 1 GND, pin 2 +12 V, pin 3 tach (open-collector, to a Pico GPIO with a 10 k pull-up to 3.3 V, C25804), pin 4 PWM from a Pico GPIO. Requires a 4-wire fan; a 2- or 3-wire fan would need a low-side MOSFET and PWM that breaks the tach. Check the chosen fan's datasheet: PWM input logic level (3.3 V compatibility) and that the tach has no internal pull-up to 12 V, which would damage the Pico GPIO. Firmware policy (starting values, not from any spec, tune on the bench): fan at a low minimum when the module NTC passes ~40 C, ramp to 100% by ~70 C; limit or cut SSR duty when the SSR heat-sink NTC passes the SSR limit (earlier assumed ~80 C base) and trip well below the ESC's own 100 C protection. ## LM5050-1 datasheet facts (TI SNVS629F, `datasheets/lm5050-1.pdf`) (part NOT used; kept as the reason the ideal diode was dropped)
- IN 1-75 V (VS 5-75 V, normally tied to OUT; 100 V abs max). GATE is charged by a 32 uA charge pump to ~12 V above IN, so Vgs ~12 V and a standard 4 V-threshold FET is fine (datasheet prefers logic-level but allows others). Turn-on time ~ Qg / 32 uA (about 2.8 ms for the 90 nC CRSS042N10N). OFF pin max 5.5 V; OFF high forces the FET off.
- FET orientation: source to IN (module), drain to OUT (bus); at power-up current flows through the body diode.
- Forward: regulates the FET drop to 22 mV at light load. Reverse: if V(IN-OUT) falls below -28 mV typ (-16 to -41 mV over temperature) it dumps the gate (25 ns detect, ~180 ns for a 47 nF gate, 2.8 A peak sink).
- **Reverse trip current = 28 mV / Rds(on).** Datasheet MOSFET guideline: 22 mV <= Id x Rds(on) <= 100 mV at nominal load current (for ~6 A, about 3.7-17 mOhm).
- **Consequence for ESC back-feed:** with the 4.2 mOhm CRSS042N10N the FET only turns off at ~6.7 A of reverse current (3.8-9.8 A), above any plausible ESC regen. Below the trip current the FET stays on in reverse, the module's output cap is pulled up with the bus, and the module OVP can still latch. A 15 mOhm FET (e.g. YJQ40G10A, C919608, DFN3333, 100 V, 15 mOhm at 10 V) trips at ~1.9 A (1.1-2.7 A) and costs ~0.7-1.0 W at 6.7 A, which still leaves gentle regen unblocked. The controller is designed for supply-short reverse transients, not for blocking gentle back-drive.
- A real diode blocks unconditionally. Schottky candidates (SMD, 10 A): SK104C (C18199211, SMC, 40 V, 550 mV at 10 A, preferred, 23,125 in stock) or SL1045 (C65021, TO-277, 45 V, 450 mV at 10 A, extended, 35,709). Expect ~0.4-0.5 V drop at 6 A, so ~2.5-3 W and a bus of ~11.5 V; needs a large copper pour (thermal estimate not verified).

## SL1045 datasheet facts (`datasheets/SL1045L000S045.pdf`, NextGen distributor sheet for MDD SL1045)
- 45 V / 10 A average, IFSM 175 A, VF 0.44 V typ / 0.48 V max at 10 A (25 C), 0.42 V at 10 A at 150 C; reverse leakage 100 uA typ / 200 uA max at 25 C and 10 mA typ / 20 mA max at 100 C. Pad layout 3.6 / 5.35 / 1.5 / 1.85 / 4.3 mm; 3 pads, TO-277.
- **RthetaJA = 65 C/W**, measured on a 2 oz board with an 18.8 x 14.4 mm cathode pad and 5.6 x 14.4 mm anode pad.
- Forward curve (Fig. 2, read off the plot, approximate): about 0.42 V at 3 A, 0.435 V at 5 A, 0.46 V at 10 A. It is nearly flat, so dissipation is about 0.43-0.45 V x I regardless of device size.
- **Thermal result:** at 5.9 A (spin coater worst case) about 2.6 W and at 6.7 A about 3.0 W; at 65 C/W that is +170 to +195 C, which exceeds the 150 C junction limit even at 25 C ambient. Even an assumed 40 C/W (large two-layer pour with vias, not verified) gives +105 to +120 C. The datasheet's own derating figure (10 A allowed at 40 C ambient) contradicts its RthetaJA and should not be trusted.
- **Paralleling is a poor fix:** the nearly flat Vf curve means a 30 mV Vf mismatch can swing the current split by roughly 2-3x, so two devices do not share evenly.
- **Topology improvement:** put only the ESC branch behind the Schottky and keep the bucks and the 12 V taps directly on the module rail. Heat is then set by ESC current only, the taps stay at 12.0 V (no 0.45 V drop) and the 12 V taps are never exposed to the TVS-clamp event.

## TPS2553 datasheet facts (from TI datasheet)
- 2.5-6.5 V input, adjustable current limit 75 mA to 1.7 A (typ) via an ILIM resistor from 15 to 232 kOhm, constant-current mode on overcurrent (the "-1" suffix is the latch-off version), FAULT open-drain output asserts on overcurrent, overtemperature and reverse voltage.
- Reverse-voltage comparator trip point 95-190 mV (135 mV typ), ~5 ms response; FAULT deglitch 2-6 ms on a reverse-voltage event, 5-10 ms on overcurrent.
- Pick the ILIM resistor from the datasheet table for ~0.7 A per tap (500 mA nominal tap).
- **Capacitors (checked 2026-10-14 from `datasheets/tps2553.pdf`):** IN needs a 0.1 uF or greater ceramic close to the device (100 nF C14663 is fine). **OUT needs no capacitor**; the datasheet only suggests an electrolytic on OUT if large load transients are expected. Loads above 10 uF with under 44 ohm are inrush-limited by the part. Closes the TPS2553 output-cap item.

## Caveats on sourced data
- LCSC prices for IRM parts look unreliable. The IRM-60 variants all show $10.40, and the IRM-90-12 shows $15.93 on LCSC vs $23.60 on Digikey. Verify live before ordering.
- Stock figures come from the JLC database, Digikey/Mouser APIs, or live checks. Re-check right before ordering.
- Entries from "JLCPCB Assembly" with $0.04 prices are zero-stock placeholders. Don't use them.
- Digikey lookups intermittently error out (3558 and 81600000005-adjacent lookups failed once); retry or use Mouser.
- jlc_search spec filters sometimes mis-parse queries (returning empty `results` with a nonzero `total`). jlc_stock_check by part number is the reliable fallback.
- Poppler is broken on this machine (missing `libgpgmepp`). Ghostscript renders PDFs fine.
